Updated 29 Oct 2026.

Pick a personal AI agent by how it controls risky actions and whether you can get it today, not by the brand on the app.

All four agents now have their own cloud computer and keep working after you close the laptop. What separates them is who says yes before a send or a payment, and whether your plan and country can get in.

That is my claim. Prices and access below are as of 29 Oct 2026. Prices can change.

The deeper posts are [Claude Duet](/blog/claude-duet-personal-ai-agent), [Grok Bot](/blog/grok-bot-solo-founders), [OpenAI Dots](/blog/openai-dots-solo-founders), and [Meta Muse](/blog/meta-muse-personal-ai-agent-solo-founders).

- All four give the agent a cloud computer and keep working after you close the laptop. Each one still needs a yes before a risky step, and that yes works differently on each product.

## What all four share

The same three ideas now show up on all four products.

  <div slot="before">The agent is a chat box on your laptop.</div>
  <div slot="after">The agent has a cloud computer and keeps working after you close the laptop.</div>
</BeforeAfter>

- **A cloud computer.** [Claude Duet](https://claude.com/product/claude-duet) works on its own computer in the cloud. [Grok Bot](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/overview) Bots share one persistent cloud computer, with a browser, files, and a terminal. A [dot](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001530-getting-started-with-your-dot) has its own cloud computer and browser. [Muse](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/) runs on Muse Secure VM, an isolated Linux machine with a browser.
- **Background work.** Duet keeps working while you are away. Grok Bot work continues when the laptop is closed. A dot keeps making progress between conversations. Muse can continue after you close the app.
- **A yes before the risky step.** Duet waits before a send, a payment, a signature, or a delete. Grok Bot shows an approval card. A dot follows rules you write, and a draft is not a send. Muse asks you to confirm a step such as sending an email or making a purchase, and a separate Sentinel agent is the only one that can allow connector actions and traffic leaving the machine.

## Shape: one agent, team, or consumer app

**Claude Duet is one proactive agent.** It sends a morning brief, then checks connected accounts every few hours. You message it. It comes back when a task is done or needs you. The [product page](https://claude.com/product/claude-duet) and the [help center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/17631495-get-started-with-claude-duet) describe that rhythm.

**OpenAI Dots start as one primary dot.** You name it. The [launch post](https://openai.com/index/introducing-dots/) describes more dots as later, and specialist dots (their own identity, credentials, and organization responsibilities) as a preview in focused enterprise pilots.

**Grok Bot is a team.** You create named Bots. They share one computer, run in parallel, and can pass a task to each other. The [overview](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/overview) and the [Cursor docs](https://cursor.com/docs/grok-bot) describe that.

**Meta Muse is a consumer agent.** It remembers what matters to you and can suggest something when you did not ask. The [launch post](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/) says that. The [safety post](https://research.meta.ai/blog/security-and-safety-for-ai-agents-our-approach-with-muse) says it can run subagents, smaller agents it starts to help with the work.

## Control and approvals

Who says yes is the part that matters when money or a message can leave your account.

<Figure
  src="/blog/grok-bot-vs-openai-dots/inline-1.webp"
  alt="Comic pop-art panel of four machines in a row. The signs read WAIT FOR OK with a red X, ALLOW ONCE with a green check, YOUR RULES with a blue gear, and SENTINEL with a yellow shield"
  caption="WAIT FOR OK, ALLOW ONCE, YOUR RULES, SENTINEL. Four ways to say yes."
/>

**Claude Duet: wait for your ok.** Before it sends a message, makes a payment, signs a document, or deletes anything, it shows you the action and waits. A separate checker runs outside the model that is doing the work. Some actions stay off limits even if you approve them. The [safety article](https://claude.com/resources/articles/claude-duet-safety-privacy-and-security) covers that gate.

**Grok Bot: allow once.** When an action needs approval, you see the operation and the inputs. The choices are Allow once, Always allow, or Deny. Auto Review is an independent review model. It can allow the action, ask you, or deny it. You can also tell a Bot to leave drafts for you to approve. The [security docs](https://cursor.com/docs/grok-bot/security) describe the cards and Auto Review. The [overview](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/overview) uses "leave me drafts to approve" as the pattern for outgoing copy.

**OpenAI Dots: your rules.** Custom Rules are optional. The four choices are: take action without asking, take action when you say so, ask before taking action, or hand the step back to you. An automatic review sits on top. It checks the action against your instructions, your rules, and the built-in safety requirements. Asking for a draft does not give permission to send. A rule does not grant access to an app or a computer, and it does not remove a confirmation the product still requires. The [controls docs](https://learn.chatgpt.com/codex/dots/controls) and the [getting started guide](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001530-getting-started-with-your-dot) both say so.

**Meta Muse: Sentinel.** The [safety post](https://research.meta.ai/blog/security-and-safety-for-ai-agents-our-approach-with-muse) says a separate Sentinel agent is the only permission authority for connector actions and for traffic leaving the machine, and that the model cannot override Sentinel. The [privacy help page](https://www.meta.com/help/superintelligence/1047255454427887/) says Muse asks you to confirm important actions, such as sending an email or making a purchase, and that many of those checks run outside the model.

## Where work runs

- **Claude Duet** uses a cloud virtual machine with its own browser. You cannot watch that browser or take it over yet. The [launch article](https://claude.com/resources/articles/claude-duet) says Duet cannot show you the browser yet.
- **Grok Bot** uses one persistent cloud computer per account, with a browser, files, and a terminal. Every Bot on that account can see the shared files, browser sessions, and app logins. The [overview](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/overview) says that. The [security docs](https://cursor.com/docs/grok-bot/security) say those computers run in the United States.
- **OpenAI Dots** give the dot its own cloud computer and browser. You can inspect it and choose Take over, then Return control. A local computer is optional and starts turned off. The [computers and apps docs](https://learn.chatgpt.com/codex/dots/computers-and-apps) and the [getting started guide](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001530-getting-started-with-your-dot) describe that.
- **Meta Muse** runs on Muse Secure VM, a dedicated computer in the cloud with its own browser. The [launch post](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/) names that VM. The [safety post](https://research.meta.ai/blog/security-and-safety-for-ai-agents-our-approach-with-muse) says each VM is an isolated Linux machine, and that you can inspect the browser work and take over.

## Where you talk to them

- **Claude Duet:** an iOS app, an Android app, and claude.ai on the web. The [product page](https://claude.com/product/claude-duet) lists those.
- **Grok Bot:** a desktop app on macOS, Windows, and Linux, plus iPhone and Android. The [overview](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/overview) lists those apps.
- **OpenAI Dots:** ChatGPT on the web, in the desktop app, and in the mobile app, plus Slack and Microsoft Teams. Mobile web is not supported. The [channels docs](https://learn.chatgpt.com/codex/dots/channels) list texting as coming soon. The [getting started guide](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001530-getting-started-with-your-dot) describes a limited beta for Pro users in the US, through a third-party provider, and says that beta is not on Business or Enterprise.
- **Meta Muse:** the Muse app on iOS and Android, muse.ai, and WhatsApp. The [launch post](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/) lists those. The [Singapore post](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/10/muse-is-now-available-in-singapore-the-worlds-first-personal-ai-agent-built-for-everyone/) also lists iOS, Android, and muse.ai.

## Access and price as of 29 Oct 2026

Prices can change. Check the vendor page before you pay.

**Claude Duet** is a research preview in the US only, on a waitlist, by invitation. You need Claude Pro or Max. During the preview it is included with those plans. These pages do not list a separate Duet price. Team and Enterprise plans are not included yet. The [product page](https://claude.com/product/claude-duet) states that access.

**Grok Bot** is included on The X Subscription at Premium ($30 a month), Super ($100 a month), and Ultra ($200 a month). Plus and Free do not include it. The free trial is a usage credit with a 7-day window. List prices are on [The X Subscription pricing](https://docs.x.ai/x-subscription/pricing) and [x.ai/pricing](https://x.ai/pricing). The [plans page](https://cursor.com/help/grok-bot/plans) describes the trial. Paid individual Cursor plans and Cursor Teams also include Grok Bot. That same plans page lists the full matrix.

**OpenAI Dots.** The first dot is included on Pro and on Business Premium, at no extra cost. Pro is $100, $200, or $500 a month, for people 18 and older, outside the EEA, the UK, and Switzerland. These pages do not list a price for extra dots, and they do not list a separate Business Premium seat price. Enterprise, including Edu and Healthcare, is a beta that an admin turns on. It stays off until then. The [getting started guide](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001530-getting-started-with-your-dot) states the access rules. [Codex pricing](https://learn.chatgpt.com/codex/pricing) lists the three Pro prices.

**Meta Muse** has a free tier with a usage limit that refreshes. Power is $20 a month and includes 500 million Muse tokens a week. Maximum is $100 a month and includes 3 billion Muse tokens a week. These pages do not say what one Muse token maps to in a real task. Muse is in limited testing in the US, Canada, Japan, and Singapore. The [subscription help page](https://www.meta.com/help/subscriptions/1021145227643680/) lists the prices. Country pages: the [launch post](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/) (US and Canada), the [Japan post](https://about.fb.com/ja/news/2026/10/muse-personal-ai-agent-launches-in-japan/), and the [Singapore post](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/10/muse-is-now-available-in-singapore-the-worlds-first-personal-ai-agent-built-for-everyone/) (27 October 2026).

## Limits and who each one fits

A few boundaries are easy to miss.

**Claude Duet** checks on a schedule, so it can miss something that arrives between checks. It does not make phone calls, and it does not send text messages to other people. Team and Enterprise plans are not included yet. It fits a person in the US who is already on Claude Pro or Max and wants one agent that briefs them and stops before send, pay, sign, or delete. The [launch article](https://claude.com/resources/articles/claude-duet) and the [product page](https://claude.com/product/claude-duet) state those limits.

**Grok Bot** puts every Bot on one computer. Treat files, browser sessions, and logins on that computer as visible to every Bot. A network allowlist and enforced Auto Review are Enterprise only. It fits work that splits into clear areas, when you are willing to treat the computer as shared. The [overview](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/overview) and the [security docs](https://cursor.com/docs/grok-bot/security) state that.

**OpenAI Dots.** A rule does not grant access to an app or a computer, and it does not remove a confirmation the product still requires. Stopping work does not undo a completed action. Deleting the dot does not recall a message already sent. It fits work that already lives in ChatGPT, Slack, or Teams, when you want to write the rules yourself. The [controls docs](https://learn.chatgpt.com/codex/dots/controls) state that.

**Meta Muse** caps every plan with a weekly token allowance. These pages do not say what one Muse token maps to in a real task. Training on chats is on when you first use Muse. You can turn it off under Settings, Data controls. A Confidential VM, meant to keep the machine private even from Meta, is described as a future choice. It is not live, and these pages do not give a date. It fits someone in one of the four countries who wants a free starting tier, WhatsApp, and a guard that sits outside the model. The [privacy help page](https://www.meta.com/help/superintelligence/1047255454427887/) states the training default and the Confidential VM. The [subscription page](https://www.meta.com/help/subscriptions/1021145227643680/) states the weekly caps. The [safety post](https://research.meta.ai/blog/security-and-safety-for-ai-agents-our-approach-with-muse) states the Sentinel limit.

## Which one should you pick?

- **Pick Claude Duet** if you are in the US on Claude Pro or Max and want one agent that briefs you each morning and stops before send, pay, sign, or delete.
- **Pick Grok Bot** if your work splits into clear areas and you want named teammates that run in parallel on one computer, with approval cards you can set per action.
- **Pick OpenAI Dots** if your work lives in ChatGPT, Slack, or Teams and you want to write your own rules for what the agent does alone.
- **Pick Meta Muse** if you are in one of its four countries, want a low entry price and WhatsApp access, and value a guard that sits outside the model.
